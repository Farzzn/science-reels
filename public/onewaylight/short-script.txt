Nobody in human history has ever measured the one-way speed of light.

Every measurement ever made bounces light off a mirror in a two-way round trip — because timing light in one direction requires two synchronized clocks, and synchronizing clocks requires already knowing light's one-way speed.

So how strange can it get?

Imagine calling Mars, ten light-minutes away. Light could crawl out at half speed for twenty minutes, and teleport back instantaneously in zero seconds — yet every clock on Earth and Mars would still read twelve-twenty!

In nineteen oh five, Einstein didn't prove equal light speed — he chose it by definition.

Even wilder: the moment you walk at five kilometers per hour, your slice of "now" tilts across space — shifting "right now" on Andromeda by eight full days!
