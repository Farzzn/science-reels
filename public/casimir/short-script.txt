Put two uncharged metal mirrors nanometers apart in empty space, at absolute zero. No air. No electricity. Almost zero gravity. Yet the moment you let go, they slam together.

What is pushing inside nothing?

In quantum physics, empty space isn't empty. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle keeps invisible electromagnetic waves humming everywhere at once.

Between two close mirrors, only tiny standing waves fit, like a pinned guitar string. Long waves are banned inside.

Outside, every wavelength hammers the outer faces. Fewer waves inside than outside pushes the mirrors together.

It's called the Casimir effect. It's strong enough to weld microscopic smartphone switches shut, and inside a special liquid, it can even push upward and levitate gold.
