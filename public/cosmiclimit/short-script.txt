Einstein proved nothing can travel faster than light — yet right now, the James Webb Telescope is watching real galaxies racing away from Earth at more than twice light speed.

How doesn't that shatter relativity? Because those galaxies aren't rocketing through space. Space itself is stretching!

Picture an ant whose absolute top speed is one centimeter per second on a rubber band. Pull the band apart at five centimeters per second — the ant never breaks its speed limit, yet the far end moves away five times faster.

General relativity puts no speed limit on empty space expanding.

Even wilder: dark energy is accelerating that stretch, locking in a cosmic event horizon sixteen-point-five billion light-years away. That means roughly ninety-five percent of the galaxies we can see tonight are already gone from our reach forever!
