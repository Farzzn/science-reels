The universe is only thirteen-point-eight billion years old — yet right now, our telescopes can see a real galaxy sitting thirty-three-point-eight billion light-years away.

Wait — how is that even possible?

Even wilder: light leaving a galaxy fifteen-point-five billion light-years away today actually starts moving backward! Because space there is expanding faster than light, the photon gets dragged farther away from Earth even while racing toward us at full light speed.

So how can it ever reach us? Because our Hubble Sphere — the boundary where space hits light speed — is growing outward.

Eventually that boundary overtakes the stranded photon, pulls it into slower-expanding space, and guides it straight to Earth.

And the galaxy we see at thirty-three-point-eight billion light-years? That is its thirteen-point-five-billion-year-old baby photo!
