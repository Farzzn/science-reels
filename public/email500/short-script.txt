In the nineteen-nineties, a university department called their sysadmin with an impossible complaint: "We can't send email more than five hundred miles."

Not "email is broken." It worked perfectly inside a five-hundred-mile circle around the campus. Outside that circle, every single message failed.

How can an email run out of distance?

When he logged into the server to test it himself, nearby universities connected instantly. Distant servers failed on the spot.

Then he noticed a recent software update had quietly downgraded the mail program, wiping out its timeout setting to zero. That meant the server only waited about three milliseconds for a reply before giving up.

And how far can a signal travel at the speed of light in three milliseconds? Right around five hundred miles.
