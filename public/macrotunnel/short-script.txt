Quantum tunneling is supposed to happen only inside invisible atoms.

Yet the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physics honored a circuit big enough to hold in your hand.

Cool a superconducting chip close to absolute zero, and billions of electrons lock into one synchronized quantum wave.

Send current through a tiny barrier inside the chip, and that collective state gets trapped at strictly zero voltage — like a marble stuck behind a hill.

Then, with zero added energy, the entire circuit tunnels straight through the hill and snaps into a live voltage spike.

Even wilder: hit it with microwaves, and that hand-sized chip absorbs energy in uneven quantum rungs — the exact physics powering modern superconducting quantum computers.
