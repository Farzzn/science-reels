In September twenty thirteen, a Super Mario Sixty-Four speedrunner was racing through Tick Tock Clock when his character suddenly teleported straight through a solid ceiling without pressing jump.

A famous analyst offered a one-thousand-dollar bounty to anyone who could repeat it on a real Nintendo console. Thousands tried. Not a single person succeeded.

When programmers worked backward from the console's memory, they found that flipping just one single bit in Mario's height byte, changing hexadecimal C-five into C-four, recreated the teleport frame for frame.

Nothing in the game's code could write that number. Instead, a subatomic particle from an exploding star in deep space had crashed through the roof and physically flipped a single microscopic memory bucket inside the chip.
